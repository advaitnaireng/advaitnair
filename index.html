About

I'm a first-year civil engineering student at UT Austin. The problems I keep coming back to are about how parts and structures carry load, and how you prove it on a test stand.

Wind turbine blade material selection

Independent project in R

View on GitHub

What

  • Built a multi-criteria model comparing aluminum alloy, fiberglass composite, and carbon composite for wind turbine blades.
  • Asked which material wins when priorities shift between performance and cost, and why industry still defaults to fiberglass.

How

  • Calculated specific stiffness and specific strength so each material is judged per unit weight, not in absolute terms.
  • Normalized density, stiffness, fatigue, and cost to a 0 to 1 scale, then combined them with a weighted score across three scenarios.
  • Documented the limits: composites treated as isotropic and fatigue as a simplified score, so this is a screening tool, not a design method.
Result: carbon composite scored highest when performance or balance mattered most (0.90 and 0.85), but fiberglass overtook it once cost was weighted heavily (0.67 vs 0.60).View the R code on GitHub
